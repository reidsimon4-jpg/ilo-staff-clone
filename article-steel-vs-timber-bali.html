Steel Frame vs Timber Frame for a Bali Villa

How the two compare in heat, humidity and termites.

Timber framing is familiar and, in some places, widely used. In Bali it faces three problems: termites, humidity and rot. Light gauge steel (LGS) is a different material for the same job. This guide compares the two for a Bali villa in plain English. It does not replace your engineer, who sets the design for your plot.

What are we comparing?

A timber frame uses wood studs, beams and rafters to hold up the walls, floors and roof. An LGS frame uses cold-formed galvanised steel: C-shaped studs and tracks for walls, C-section joists for floors, and steel trusses for the roof. ILO makes its frames in its own factory in Banyuwangi, Java. For a simple explanation of the system, see what is light gauge steel.

Termites

Termites are one of the biggest threats to timber buildings in Indonesia and South East Asia. Timber builds often rely on chemical soil treatments to keep them out. Steel is inorganic, so it gives termites and other insects nothing to eat. A steel frame does not need those treatments.

Humidity, mould and rot

Bali is hot and humid for much of the year, and it has a monsoon season. Timber can degrade quickly in this climate. Steel cannot grow mould, mildew or rot. LGS is also dimensionally stable: it does not expand or contract with moisture or temperature the way timber does, so there is less warping.

Rust: the fair question about steel

Steel can rust if it is not protected. LGS carries a heavy zinc or aluminium-zinc coating for this reason. When the frame is installed and enclosed to engineering standards, its structural lifespan is more than 50 years, even in the tropics. The quality of the install matters, so ask any builder how the frame will be enclosed and protected. Our guide to termites, humidity and rust goes further.

Fire

Timber burns. LGS is non-combustible: it does not ignite, burn or add fuel to a fire. For developers this may also help with insurance costs.

Weight and straightness

LGS weighs less than wood studs and keeps its structure without settling or sagging. Frames made in a factory with CAD/CAM roll-forming machines are accurate to the millimetre, so the parts fit together on site. See our factory for how the frames are made.

Forests and recycling

Using steel reduces demand for timber and helps protect Indonesia's rainforests. Steel contains at least 25% recycled material and is 100% recyclable. Waste on site is about 2%, compared with about 20% for conventional methods. More in our sustainability article.

Where timber still has a place

Many people love the warmth of timber, and nothing stops you using it. Timber can be used for decks, screens, doors, ceilings and other features on a steel frame villa. Cladding, render and stone can also be added over the frame. The look is a design choice, not a limit of the structure.

Questions to ask any builder

  • How is the frame protected from moisture and enclosed?

Frequently asked questions

Is a steel frame better than a timber frame in Bali?

In Bali's heat and humidity, steel has clear advantages on termites, rot, mould and fire. Timber can degrade quickly in humidity, and termites are one of the biggest threats to timber buildings in Indonesia and South East Asia. Steel is inorganic, so termites have nothing to eat and mould and rot cannot grow.

Do steel frames need termite treatment?

No. Steel gives termites no food source, so a steel frame does not need the chemical soil treatments common in tropical timber builds.

Will a steel frame rust in Bali's humidity?

The steel carries a heavy zinc or aluminium-zinc coating that protects it from corrosion. When a frame is installed and enclosed to engineering standards, its structural lifespan is more than 50 years, even in the tropics.

Does steel warp or sag like timber?

LGS is dimensionally stable. It does not expand or contract with moisture content or temperature changes the way timber does. It also weighs less than wood studs and keeps its structure without settling or sagging.

Can a steel frame villa still have timber features?

Yes. The frame is the structure. Cladding, render, stone and interior finishes are added on top, so the look of the villa is a design choice, not a limit of the frame.
