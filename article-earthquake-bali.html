Steel frame vs concrete in Bali's earthquake zone

How light steel and concrete behave differently when the ground shakes.

Bali sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire. Every villa owner and investor here should think about earthquakes before choosing how to build. This article explains, in plain words, how a light steel frame and a concrete or block building behave differently when the ground shakes.

If steel framing is new to you, read What is LGS? first. LGS is light gauge steel, a frame made from thin galvanised steel sections.

The short answer

A steel frame is much lighter than concrete. Earthquake forces rise with building weight, so a light building attracts lower forces. Steel is also ductile. It bends and absorbs energy instead of cracking suddenly. These two traits are why light gauge steel is a sound choice in a seismic zone.

It is not magic. Any building still needs proper engineering for its site.

Why weight matters

When the ground shakes, a building is pushed sideways. The push depends on how much mass the building has. Think of a heavy truck and a small car braking at the same speed. The truck needs far more force to stop.

A concrete or block villa is heavy. Its walls, columns, slabs and roof all carry mass. In an earthquake that mass is shaken sideways, and the structure and foundations must resist large forces.

A steel frame villa is far lighter. The same shaking produces smaller forces, so the structure has less to resist. You can see more on the weight and speed benefits on the LGS benefits page.

Why ductile steel behaves better than brittle walls

Ductile means a material can bend a long way before it breaks. Steel does this. Under heavy loading it flexes and absorbs energy. It tends to give warning before it fails.

Unreinforced block work is brittle. It is strong when squeezed but weak when pushed sideways or pulled. Under strong shaking it can crack and fail suddenly. Good reinforcement and detailing improve concrete and block work. The point is that the way a material fails matters as much as how strong it is.

Ductility also gives people more time to get out. That matters most for a rental villa with guests who do not know the building.

Where the frame is only as good as its connections

A frame is a system, not a pile of parts. The walls, floors and roof must be tied together, and the whole building must be tied to its foundation. Weak connections undo the benefit of a light, ductile frame.

This is one reason factory fabrication helps. ILO makes its frames in its own factory in Banyuwangi, Java, to an approved design, with inspection points along the way. Components arrive on site marked and ready to assemble. Read more on the build process page.

What a steel frame does not change

  • Foundations still matter. The ground, the footings and the way the frame is fixed down all need engineering for your plot.
  • Heavy add-ons add weight. A stone or concrete finish adds mass. Talk to your engineer before you add it.
  • Design quality matters. A badly detailed building can perform poorly in any material.

A steel frame helps. It does not replace good design and good connections.

What this means for a Bali villa owner

If you are choosing between a concrete villa and a steel frame villa, earthquake behaviour is one point of several. Steel framing also resists termites and rot in tropical humidity, and projects are typically completed 2 to 3 times faster. Frequently asked questions

Is a steel frame safer than concrete in an earthquake?

A steel frame is much lighter than concrete, so it attracts lower earthquake forces. Steel is also ductile, so it bends and absorbs energy instead of cracking suddenly. Safety still depends on proper engineering, strong connections and a sound foundation.

Why does building weight matter in an earthquake?

Earthquake forces on a building rise with its weight. A heavier building has more mass that the shaking pushes sideways, so the walls, columns and foundations must resist larger forces. A lighter building sees smaller forces.

What does ductile mean?

Ductile means a material can bend and stretch a long way before it breaks. Steel gives warning by deforming. Brittle materials such as unreinforced block work can crack and fail suddenly.

Does a steel frame villa still need an engineer?

Yes. Every building needs proper engineering for its site. A steel frame helps, but it does not replace good design, good connections and good foundations.

Can I still have a block or stone look on a steel frame villa?

Yes. The frame is the structure. Block, render, stone and other finishes can be added over it, as covered in the comparison of steel framing and block work.
