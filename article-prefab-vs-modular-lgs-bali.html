Prefab, modular or steel frame? What the terms mean in Bali

What each term means, and what it tells you about the building.

Builders in Bali use the words prefab, modular and steel frame as if they mean the same thing. They do not. If you are buying a villa, the difference affects price, speed and what you actually receive. This guide explains each term in plain English.

What does prefab mean?

Prefab is short for prefabricated. It means that parts of the building are made in a factory, then moved to site and put together. The parts might be wall panels, floor frames or roof trusses. The rest of the work, such as foundations, cladding and finishes, is done on site.

The main benefit is control. A factory is dry, level and organised. Cuts are made by machine from a drawing. Mistakes are found before the parts leave the building, not after they are fixed in place.

What does modular mean?

Modular means the building is made from repeatable sections that follow a set plan. Some builders make whole rooms in a factory and lift them into place. Others make frames in sections and join them on site. The result is the same idea: a building designed as a kit of parts rather than built from scratch.

Modular design suits developers building several villas or townhouses to a similar plan, because each unit repeats the same parts. It also suits single villas, as long as the design is settled before manufacturing starts.

What is light gauge steel?

Light gauge steel (LGS) is the material. It is cold-formed galvanised steel, rolled into C-shaped studs, tracks and joists at room temperature. This gives a yield strength of 230-340 MPa, to ASTM standards. A zinc or aluminium-zinc coating protects it from rust. You can read more on the What is LGS? page.

So the terms sit at different levels. Prefab and modular describe how a building is made. Light gauge steel describes what the frame is made of. A villa can be prefab and modular and also use a steel frame, which is how ILO builds.

How ILO builds

ILO runs its own factory in Banyuwangi, Java. Galvanised steel coil is rolled into profiles, cut to length and assembled into wall frames, floor systems and roof trusses. The frames are packed and shipped to Bali, then erected on site. After that come cladding, internal linings, services and finishes. The full eight steps are on the process page.

Why the material matters in Bali

Prefab is only as good as what it is made from. Three Bali conditions put pressure on the material:

  • Termites. Steel is inorganic, so termites have nothing to eat. It also cannot rot or grow mould.
  • Humidity. Galvanised coating protects the steel in the tropical climate, and the frame does not warp.
  • Earthquakes. Steel framing is much lighter than concrete, so it attracts lower earthquake forces. It is also ductile, so it flexes and absorbs energy.

A prefab frame made of untreated timber would still face the termite and humidity problems. This is why many buyers ask about the material before the method.

Speed and cost

Light gauge steel projects are usually finished 2 to 3 times faster than traditional methods. Total project costs are generally 25-40% lower than traditional concrete and brick once speed, labour and waste are counted. Construction waste is about 2%, compared with about 20% for conventional methods.

Price is set by build stage. A frame and roof package starts from about Rp 2.5 million per square metre. A weather-tight lockup shell is about Rp 4 million per square metre. A turnkey villa typically runs Rp 7-11 million per square metre, depending on finish level and location. The article on how much a steel frame villa costs in Bali explains each stage.

Questions to ask any prefab builder

  • Is the frame made in your own factory, or bought in?
  • What is the frame made of, and how is it protected?
  • Does the quote cover frame and roof, lockup or turnkey?
  • Who handles permits and site management?

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between prefab and modular construction?

Prefab means parts of the building are made in a factory and finished on site. Modular usually means the building is made in repeatable sections to a set plan. The two terms overlap, and many builders use them for the same method.

Is light gauge steel prefab or modular?

Light gauge steel is a material, not a method. It can be used for either. ILO rolls the steel into frames in its own factory in Banyuwangi, Java, builds wall, floor and roof frames, then ships them to site for assembly.

Is a prefab villa in Bali less durable than a block villa?

Not by nature. Durability depends on the material and the design. A galvanised steel frame cannot be eaten by termites, and when properly installed and enclosed it has a structural lifespan of more than 50 years in the tropics.

Are prefab villas faster to build in Bali?

Yes. Light gauge steel projects are usually finished 2 to 3 times faster than traditional methods. The factory work runs at the same time as site preparation, and the frame can be erected on site in days.

How do I compare prefab quotes with block work quotes?

Compare scope first. Ask whether the price covers frame and roof, a weather-tight lockup shell, or a complete turnkey villa. Published ILO tiers are from about Rp 2.5 million per square metre for frame and roof, about Rp 4 million for lockup, and Rp 7-11 million for turnkey.
