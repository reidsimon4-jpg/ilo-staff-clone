Termites, humidity and rust: how steel frames hold up in the tropics

Termites, humidity and rust, and what keeps a galvanised frame protected.

Termites, constant humidity and rust are the three things villa owners in Bali worry about most when they look at a steel frame. This article explains, in plain words, how a galvanised steel frame deals with each one, and where a frame still needs good design and care.

If steel framing is new to you, read What is LGS? first. LGS is light gauge steel, a frame made from thin galvanised steel sections.

The short answer

Steel is inorganic, so termites cannot eat it and mould cannot grow in it. It does not rot, swell or warp in humid air. The one real risk is rust, and that is handled by a heavy zinc or aluminium-zinc coating. Installed and enclosed to engineering standards, an LGS frame has a structural lifespan of more than 50 years, even in Indonesia's tropical climate.

Termites

Termites are one of the biggest threats to timber buildings in Indonesia and South East Asia. They feed on wood and on other plant material. Steel gives them no food source at all.

That means a steel frame villa does not need the toxic chemical soil treatments that are common with timber construction. Termites can still be a nuisance in non-structural materials such as timber doors or furniture, but they cannot damage the frame that holds the building up.

Humidity and mould

Bali is warm and humid for most of the year. Timber takes up moisture in that air. It can swell, warp, rot and grow mould. Steel does not take up moisture. It is dimensionally stable, which means it does not expand or shrink with moisture or with temperature changes.

A steel frame cannot support mould or mildew itself. Mould can still grow on damp surfaces like plasterboard or paint, so a villa still needs ventilation and a dry wall build-up. The frame removes one source of trouble. It does not replace good design.

Rust and the galvanised coating

Steel rusts when bare steel meets water and air. LGS studs are made from galvanised sheet steel, which carries a coating of zinc or aluminium-zinc. The coating keeps water away from the steel underneath.

The steel is then cold-formed. This means it is rolled into C-shaped sections at room temperature, with no heat. The process also makes the steel stronger. For more on how the frame is made, see the LGS guide and the factory page.

ILO states that a properly installed and enclosed frame has a structural lifespan of more than 50 years in Indonesia's tropical climate. The word that matters is enclosed. The coating does its job best when the frame is kept inside the wall, floor and roof build-up and not left exposed to the weather.

Where a steel frame still needs care

Steel framing is not maintenance free. These are the points a buyer should ask any builder about:

  • Enclosure. A frame left open to rain for a long time is not in the condition the design assumes. Ask how soon the building is made weather-tight.
  • Cut ends and fixings. Cuts and drilled holes on site should be treated and checked. Factory-cut parts reduce the amount of cutting done on site.
  • Trapped moisture. Walls and floors need a sound waterproofing and drainage layout so that water does not sit against the steel.
  • Finishes and roofing. These still need normal upkeep on any villa.

Frequently asked questions

Do termites eat steel frames?

No. Steel is inorganic, so it gives termites and other insects nothing to eat. This also removes the need for the chemical soil treatments common in tropical timber builds.

Will a steel frame rust in Bali's humidity?

The studs carry a heavy zinc or aluminium-zinc coating that protects the steel from corrosion. ILO states a structural lifespan of more than 50 years in Indonesia's tropical climate when the frame is installed and enclosed to engineering standards.

Can mould grow on a steel frame?

No. Steel cannot support mould, mildew or rot, and it does not warp or swell in high humidity the way timber can. Mould can still grow on damp surfaces such as plasterboard, so a dry, well-ventilated building matters.

What damages a steel frame if it is installed badly?

Moisture trapped against the steel for a long time, cut ends left unprotected, and frames left open to the weather are the main risks. This is why enclosure and good detailing matter, and why ILO frames are made in a factory and installed to an approved design.

Is a steel frame villa lower maintenance than a timber one?

The frame itself needs no termite treatment and does not rot. Finishes, roofing and waterproofing still need normal upkeep, as they do on any villa.
