The Eco-Friendly Future of Real Estate

Why sustainable construction with Light Gauge Steel is the new standard.

Sustainability is moving from a buzzword to something buyers, banks and regulators ask about. Developers in Indonesia are looking at light gauge steel (LGS) because it cuts waste, can be fully recycled, and helps buildings stay cooler. This article explains each point in plain terms.

If you are new to the method, read what LGS is first. In short, it is a building frame made from thin, galvanised steel sections, manufactured in a factory and assembled on site.

Steel is 100% recyclable

Concrete is carbon-intensive and difficult to recycle. Steel is one of the most recycled materials in the world. New steel contains a minimum of 25% recycled content, and a finished LGS frame can be melted down and reused at the end of a building's life without losing its structural properties.

That matters for investors. A building made of recyclable parts is easier to explain to buyers who care about the environment, and easier to defend if rules on building materials tighten.

Far less waste on site

Traditional building sites throw away a lot of material. Offcuts, broken blocks and spoiled mortar end up in landfill. Site waste on conventional projects is about 20%. With LGS it is about 2%.

The reason is simple. ILO makes its frames at its own factory in Banyuwangi, Java. Every stud, joist and truss member is cut to exact length from a drawing. The parts arrive on site ready to assemble. Pre-punched holes carry plumbing and electrical runs, so there is little drilling or cutting on site.

Shorter builds mean fewer site impacts

LGS projects are completed 2 to 3 times faster than traditional methods. A villa structure that takes months in concrete can be erected in days. Shorter builds mean fewer weeks of noise, dust, truck movements and workers on site. Neighbours and nearby guests notice the difference, which is useful when you are building next to existing villas or a resort.

A lighter structure

A steel frame is much lighter than concrete. Less weight means lower earthquake forces on the building, which is covered in the benefits page. It also means less heavy material to haul and lift. Your engineer will still set the foundation design for each site.

Energy efficiency in a tropical climate

The space inside LGS stud walls holds insulation. With a good insulation design, a villa needs less energy to cool in Bali's heat. This depends on the wall and roof assembly, including insulation and detailing to limit heat passing through the steel. It does not come from the steel structure alone. Ask your builder to show you the wall and roof layers before you sign.

Durability is a sustainability issue too

A building that lasts longer does not need to be rebuilt or repaired as often. Steel does not rot, warp or grow mould. It gives termites nothing to eat, which removes the need for chemical soil treatments common in tropical timber builds. Galvanised coatings protect against corrosion when properly installed and insulated.

Sustainability and cost go together

Green building has a reputation for costing more. With LGS the savings and the environmental gains come from the same place: less waste, less labour and a shorter build. Total project costs are generally 25-40% lower than traditional concrete and brick, once speed and waste are counted. A frame and roof package starts from about Rp 2.5 million per square metre, a lockup shell is about Rp 4 million, and a turnkey villa is typically Rp 7-11 million per square metre. Waste is a cost as well as an environmental problem. Every block that is broken or cut off has been paid for. A method that wastes about 2% instead of about 20% leaves more of your budget in the building.

What to ask any builder about sustainability

  • Where are the parts made, and how much material is wasted?
  • What is the insulation design for walls and roof?
  • Can the structure be recycled at the end of its life?
  • How long will the build take, and what impact will it have on neighbours?

Frequently asked questions

Is steel framing more sustainable than concrete?

Steel is 100% recyclable and contains a minimum of 25% recycled content. Concrete is carbon-intensive and hard to recycle. LGS also cuts site waste to about 2%, compared with about 20% for conventional methods.

Can a light gauge steel frame be recycled?

Yes. Steel can be melted down and reused at the end of a building's life without losing its structural properties.

Does a steel frame villa need less energy for cooling?

The space between LGS studs is filled with insulation. With good insulation and thermal-break detailing, a building needs less energy to cool in a tropical climate. The result depends on the insulation design, not on the steel structure itself.

How much waste does steel construction produce?

About 2% site waste, compared with about 20% for conventional methods. Parts are made to exact size in the factory, so little material is thrown away on site.

Does a lighter building use less foundation material?

A steel frame is much lighter than concrete. Lighter loads can allow simpler foundations, but your engineer sets the foundation design for each site.
