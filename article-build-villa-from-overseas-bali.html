Building a Villa in Bali From Overseas

What to ask, who handles permits and what you receive when you live abroad.

Many villa owners in Bali do not live there. They run a business in Australia, Europe or Asia and visit a few times a year. Building a villa from a distance is possible, but it needs a clear scope, one team in charge and good records. This guide covers what to ask, what to expect and where a steel frame villa can make remote ownership easier. It is general guidance. Your own site, design and contract set the details.

What goes wrong when you are not on site

The common problems are not about steel or concrete. They are about people and paperwork. A quote that leaves things out. Permits that nobody owns. Messages that go unanswered for a week. A build that runs long, with no one to tell you why. When you are on the other side of the world, each of these costs more because you cannot walk the site and sort it out in an hour.

Our guide on how to choose a villa builder in Bali lists eight plain questions to ask before you sign. Most of them matter even more if you will not be there.

Choose one team that runs the whole project

The fewer parties you manage, the easier remote ownership is. ILO is a factory-direct builder. One company handles design, the factory, transport, assembly and handover. Its process has eight steps: consultation and planning, architectural and structural design, permits and regulatory compliance, factory manufacturing, frame prefabrication, transport and logistics, on-site assembly, and finishing and handover. You can see how they fit together in the build timeline and on the construction process page.

ILO offers project management in English and Indonesian. Whichever builder you choose, ask who your single point of contact will be and what language the project is run in.

Get the scope in writing

Ask what the price includes and what it leaves out. ILO prices by build stage:

  • Frame and roof: from about Rp 2.5 million per square metre.
  • Lockup shell: about Rp 4 million per square metre, weather-tight.
  • Turnkey villa: typically Rp 7 to 11 million per square metre, complete with finishes and services.

Your own price depends on the design, floor area, number of floors and finishes. The cost guide explains what each stage covers, and the Build & Price tool gives a first estimate. When you compare builders, make sure you compare the same stage.

Name an owner for permits

Permits can stall a project if no one owns them, and an owner abroad cannot chase an office in person. Ask who applies, who tracks the approvals and what happens if the rules change. At ILO, permits and regulatory compliance are a standard step in the process, and the team works through Indonesia's permit requirements as part of the project. Rules differ by location and use, so confirm the details for your plot with the builder and your own advisers.

Why a factory-made frame suits remote owners

ILO makes its frames in its own factory in Banyuwangi, Java, to an approved design, with inspection points along the way. The frame arrives marked and ready to assemble. That moves a large part of the work from your site, where you cannot see it, to a controlled factory. Read more on the factory page.

It also shortens the time on site. Projects are typically completed 2 to 3 times faster than traditional methods, and a villa superstructure can be erected in days. A shorter build gives fewer weeks for things to drift. The total programme still depends on the design, permits, foundations and finishes, so we do not quote fixed weeks here.

The frame itself needs no termite treatment and does not rot, which means fewer worries about a villa that sits empty between visits. See termites, humidity and rust for the detail. Finishes, roofing and waterproofing still need normal upkeep, as on any villa.

What to ask for at handover

A finished villa should come with inspection and documentation. At ILO, handover includes a thorough quality inspection and full project documentation, after cladding, linings, electrical, plumbing and final finishes are done. If you cannot be there for the walk-through, ask the builder what records, photos and documents you will receive, and whether someone you trust can inspect on your behalf.

Questions to ask a builder if you live abroad

  • Who is my single point of contact, and in which language?
  • What does the quote include and exclude?
  • Who owns permits, and how will I see progress on them?
  • Where is the frame made, and what inspections happen on the way?
  • What documents will I receive at handover?
