How to choose a villa builder in Bali: 8 questions to ask

Eight plain questions to ask before you sign a contract.

Choosing a builder is the biggest decision in a villa project. Prices and promises are easy to put on paper. What matters is what sits behind them. This guide gives eight plain questions to ask any villa builder in Bali, with the answers we give for ILO so you can compare like with like.

1. What exactly does the quote include?

Two quotes can look very different in price because they cover different things. One may be a frame and roof only. Another may be a finished, furnished villa. Ask for a written list of what is in and what is out.

ILO prices in three stages. Frame and roof is from about Rp 2.5 million per square metre. A weather-tight lockup shell is about Rp 4 million per square metre. A complete turnkey villa typically runs Rp 7-11 million per square metre, depending on finish level and location. Our quoting system uses a fixed scope, so each line is clear. Read the full breakdown in how much a steel frame villa costs in Bali.

2. Who handles building permits?

Permits take time and can stall a project if no one owns them. Ask who applies, who tracks the approvals and what happens if the rules change. At ILO, permits and regulatory compliance are one of the eight steps in our process, and our team works through Indonesia's permit requirements as part of the project. You can see all eight steps in the build timeline.

3. Where are the materials made?

A builder who buys finished parts from others depends on their schedule and quality. A builder who owns the factory controls both. ILO makes its steel frames in its own factory in Banyuwangi, East Java, and ships them to Bali, Lombok and the rest of Indonesia. There are no middlemen in between. See the factory for real footage of the production floor.

4. How long will it take, and what runs at the same time?

Ask for a programme, not just a finish date. In a steel frame build, the frame is made in the factory while the site team prepares the ground and foundation. The two jobs overlap, and that is one of the main reasons the build is shorter. Steel frame projects are consistently finished 2 to 3 times faster than traditional methods, and a villa superstructure can go up in days.

Be careful with any builder who promises a date without seeing your site and design. The full programme depends on the size, the finish level and the site.

5. How does the structure handle earthquakes?

Bali sits on an active seismic belt. Ask the builder to explain, in plain words, how the structure is designed for it. A steel frame is much lighter than concrete, so it attracts lower earthquake forces, and the steel is ductile, so it flexes instead of failing suddenly. Good design still matters. Our article on steel frame vs concrete in Bali's earthquake zone explains how this works.

6. What about termites, humidity and rust?

Timber and many fit-out materials suffer in Bali's climate. A galvanised steel frame gives termites nothing to eat and cannot grow mould or rot. The zinc or aluminium-zinc coating protects against rust, and the structural lifespan exceeds 50 years when the frame is properly installed and enclosed. Ask any builder what protects each part of your villa, and read more in termites, humidity and rust.

7. Who will manage the project, and in which language?

Misunderstandings cost money. Ask who your day-to-day contact is, and whether you can talk to them in your own language. ILO offers project management in English and Indonesian.

8. What do I receive at handover?

A finished villa should come with inspection and documentation. At ILO, handover includes a thorough quality inspection and full project documentation, after cladding, linings, electrical, plumbing and final finishes are done. Ask every builder for the same list in writing.

A short checklist

  • A written, fixed-scope quote that lists inclusions and exclusions.
  • A named owner for permits.
  • Clear information on where and by whom materials are made.
  • A programme that shows what happens at the same time.
  • A plain explanation of earthquake and termite protection.
  • A project manager you can talk to easily.
  • A written handover list.

Frequently asked questions

What should I ask a villa builder in Bali before signing?

Ask what the quote includes and excludes, who handles permits, where the materials are made, how long the build takes, how the structure copes with earthquakes and termites, who manages the project in English, and what documents you receive at handover.

What is a fixed-scope quote?

It is a quote that lists exactly what is included, so you can compare it line by line with other quotes. ILO uses a fixed-scope quoting system, and prices by build stage so you can see what each stage covers.

Why does it matter where the frame is made?

A builder that owns its factory controls quality, scheduling and cost. ILO makes its steel frames in its own factory in Banyuwangi, Java, and ships them to Bali, so there is no middleman between the factory and your site.

Should I choose the cheapest quote?

Not without checking scope. Two quotes at different prices often cover different things, such as frame only against lockup or turnkey. Compare what is included at each stage before comparing the totals.

Can I get an estimate before I contact a builder?

Yes. The ILO Build & Price tool gives an instant estimate, and published price tiers start from about Rp 2.5 million per square metre for frame and roof.
