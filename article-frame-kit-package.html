Frame kits for builders: what a light gauge steel package includes

What the frame covers, what it leaves out, and what to confirm before you compare quotes.

Many builders in Bali and Lombok have not bought a steel frame before. They ask a simple question: what exactly do I get? This article explains, in plain words, what a light gauge steel (LGS) frame kit covers and what you still need to allow for. The details come from the Infinity Structural page, where ILO supplies frame packages to builders and developers.

If steel framing is new to you, read What is LGS? first. LGS is a frame made from thin galvanised steel sections.

The short answer

A frame kit is the structure only. It is the steel skeleton of the building, made in a factory to your approved drawings and delivered ready to assemble. It is not a finished building. Foundations, coverings, finishes and services are separate.

Where the kit is made

ILO roll forms the frame components in its own factory in Banyuwangi, Java. Roll forming means flat steel coil is fed through rollers that shape it into the sections used in the frame. Components then travel to Bali or Lombok for your project. You can see more about the factory on the factory page.

What is in a supply-only kit

The supply option suits builders who manage their own site assembly. The kit covers:

  • Structural panels and trusses made to the approved design.
  • Fixings listed in the agreed supply schedule.
  • Erection drawings, component identification and a packing list, so your crew can check every piece on arrival and know where it goes.

Freight, unloading and erection are scoped separately.

What supply and erect adds

Some projects also need an agreed erection scope. In that case the kit is the same project-specific structural package. On top of it, erection responsibilities are defined with your team, and access, lifting and logistics are assessed for the site. An installed quotation follows project review. The kit reference rate is not an installed rate.

What is not in the kit

This is the part that causes most confusion when builders compare quotes. A frame kit does not include:

  • Foundations and slabs.
  • Wall and roof coverings.
  • Services such as plumbing and electrical.
  • Permits and professional design.
  • Freight, ferry, unloading, cranes and erection, which need a defined allowance.
  • Upper-floor decking, toppings and stairs on a two-storey job.

Unless a quotation says otherwise, treat these as separate items. Agree the interfaces with your engineer and builder before you compare one supplier with another.

Two-storey and hybrid projects

Two-storey villas are part of the scope ILO discusses, but each one needs its own engineering and quotation. The same applies to hybrid structures. LGS can be combined with WF, SHS or RHS steel members where the design needs them. Those hybrid connections and members are separately engineered and quoted.

Site access matters

Remote and steep sites need thought early. Roads, unloading, lifting and assembly space all affect the delivery and erection plan. Tell ILO about access when you send your drawings. It saves surprises later.

How the process runs

  1. Share the project. Send drawings, location, floor area and storey count, plus your preferred supply or erect scope.
  2. Define and engineer. Review the structural boundary with your project professionals. Confirm drawings, inclusions and separate allowances.
  3. Quote and confirm. Agree a project-specific quotation and supply schedule. Factory timing starts after deposit and approved design, subject to confirmation.
  4. Manufacture and coordinate. The agreed kit is roll formed in Banyuwangi, then dispatch, site logistics and erection responsibilities are coordinated for your scope.

Price and timing: what is published

For a single-storey kit, ILO publishes a planning reference of about Rp 660,000 per square metre of floor area, ex-factory. It is based on an 11 kg/m² planning assumption. It is a starting point for a conversation, not a final price. Two-storey, installed and hybrid packages are quoted per project. The reference factory lead time is 4 weeks after deposit and approved design. It is not a delivery promise, and dispatch, transport and site assembly need their own plan.

Frequently asked questions

What is a light gauge steel frame kit?

A frame kit is the structural steel frame of a building, roll formed in a factory and supplied as panels and trusses to an approved design. It comes with erection drawings, marked components and a packing list. It is not a finished building.

What does an ILO frame kit include?

Structural panels and trusses to the approved design, fixings listed in the agreed supply schedule, and erection drawings with component identification and a packing list. Freight, unloading and erection are scoped separately.

What is not included in a frame kit?

Foundations and slabs, wall and roof coverings, services, permits and professional design are outside the kit unless expressly agreed. Freight, ferry, unloading, cranes and erection need their own allowance. Upper-floor decking, toppings and stairs are not automatically included in a two-storey kit.

Can ILO supply a two-storey frame?

Yes. Two-storey villas are part of the scope ILO discusses. The frame, floor interfaces and any hybrid steel need project-specific engineering and a project-specific quotation.

What should I send to get a frame kit quote?

Send architectural drawings or a concept plan, the project location, total floor area, storey count and whether you want supply only or supply and erect. Site access details help ILO discuss logistics and erection.
