Incorporating Prefab into Tropical Villas

How high-end Balinese architecture is adopting modular components.

Balinese villas are known for their design and their link to the landscape. Building them has always meant a lot of hand work on site. Many architects now use a factory-made light gauge steel (LGS) frame for the structure and keep the hand work for the finishes. This article explains what that means for a villa design, in plain terms.

If steel framing is new to you, read What is LGS? first.

What prefab means for a villa

Prefab does not mean a standard box. It means the structure is made in a factory and assembled on site. ILO rolls the steel sections in its own factory in Banyuwangi, East Java. The wall frames, floor sections and roof trusses are built there and sent to site ready to assemble. The architect still designs the villa. The factory builds the frame to the drawings.

Precision and straight lines

The frame is made from CAD drawings on computer-controlled roll-forming machines. Parts are cut to length to millimetre precision. That gives straight walls and square openings. Straight walls matter for the features modern tropical design relies on: flat roofs, large glass doors, long sliding panels and clean edges where materials meet.

A real example is on the factory page. One wall panel used 36.5 m of steel with only 153 mm of scrap, about 0.4%. Parts that fit first time also mean fewer gaps to hide with extra render or trim.

Design freedom with a steel frame

The frame is the structure only. It does not decide how the villa looks. Cladding, render, stone, timber details and interior finishes go on top. The same frame can sit under a plain white modern villa or a villa with traditional materials.

Steel is also strong for its weight. That helps with open plans and wide openings. As a guide, the system suits the 2 to 3 storey residential buildings that are common in Bali, and it can carry mid-rise buildings up to 10 storeys. Each design still needs engineering for its own site.

Services fit into the frame too. The steel sections have pre-punched holes for electrical and plumbing runs, so installers do not need to cut into the structure.

Less disruption on tight sites

Many Bali plots are narrow, in rice-field areas or close to neighbours. A block build brings sand, cement, mixers and weeks of noise and dust. With a prefab frame, most of the cutting and fabrication happens in the factory. The frame arrives packed and is assembled on site by a crew. There is less noise, less dust and fewer heavy machines. The site also stays tidier because site waste is about 2%, compared with about 20% for conventional methods.

Speed and cost for the design team

LGS projects are typically completed 2 to 3 times faster than traditional methods. A villa superstructure that takes months in concrete can be erected in days. Foundation and site work can run while the frame is being made. Total project cost is generally 25-40% lower than concrete and brick once speed and waste are included.

Published pricing is by stage. An engineered frame and roof package starts at about Rp 2.5 million per square metre. A weather-tight lockup shell is about Rp 4 million per square metre. A turnkey villa is typically Rp 7-11 million per square metre, depending on finish level and location. The Build and Price tool gives an instant estimate.

Sustainability in the design

Architects in Bali are often asked to reduce the impact of a build. A factory frame helps in a simple way. Steel contains at least 25% recycled content and is 100% recyclable. Because parts are cut to length in the factory, less material is thrown away. The benefits page lists the other advantages, including termite resistance, which removes the need for chemical soil treatment.

How to work with a prefab builder

The best results come when the builder sees the design early. The steps are covered on the process page.

  • Share the concept design and site details at the start.
  • The ILO engineering team adapts the design to steel framing and the site.
  • Permits and approvals follow the local requirements.
  • The frame is made and prefabricated in the factory, then shipped to site.
  • The crew assembles the frame, then cladding, services and finishes go on.

Frequently asked questions

Can an architect-designed villa be built with a prefab steel frame?

Yes. The frame is made to the architect's drawings. Prefab describes how the structure is made, not how the villa looks. Cladding, stone, render and interior finishes are chosen as in any other build.

Does prefab limit the design to simple boxes?

No. Walls, floors and roof trusses are cut and formed to the design. Straight walls and square openings suit flat roofs and large glass doors. Complex shapes need early engineering, so share the design at the start.

How precise is a factory-made steel frame?

Parts are cut to millimetre precision from CAD drawings on computer-controlled roll-forming machines. One real ILO wall panel used 36.5 m of steel with only 153 mm of scrap.

How does prefab reduce disruption on a small Bali plot?

Most cutting and fabrication happens in the factory. The frame arrives ready to assemble, so there is less noise, dust and heavy machinery on site, and site waste is about 2% compared with about 20% for conventional methods.

How tall can a steel frame building be?

LGS can support mid-rise buildings up to 10 storeys. The 2 to 3 storey buildings common in Bali are well within that range. Every design still needs engineering for its site.
