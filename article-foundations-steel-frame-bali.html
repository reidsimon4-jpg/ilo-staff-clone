Foundations for a Steel Frame Villa in Bali

What the frame sits on, who designs it, and how the work overlaps.

A steel frame villa is light, but it still stands on the ground. Buyers often ask whether steel framing changes the foundation, who decides what goes in, and whether the foundation is part of the frame price. This guide answers those questions in plain words. It does not replace an engineer, because the right foundation depends on your plot.

Foundations still matter

Light gauge steel (LGS) villas still sit on a foundation, and lighter frames allow lighter foundations. A steel frame helps, but it does not replace good design, good connections and good foundations. The walls, floors and roof must be tied together, and the whole building must be tied to its foundation. If that link is weak, the frame cannot do its job.

Why a lighter building asks less of the ground

A concrete and block building has heavy walls, columns, slabs and roof. All that mass sits on the soil. A steel frame is much lighter, so it puts less load on the ground. Lighter loads can allow simpler foundations, which is one reason the site-work cost can come down. The saving is real but it is not a fixed number. Your engineer sets the foundation design for each site.

Mass matters in an earthquake too. In an earthquake that mass is shaken sideways, and the structure and foundations must resist large forces. A lighter frame means smaller forces to resist. Our guide to steel frame vs concrete in Bali's earthquake zone explains this in more detail.

What your engineer decides

The ground, the footings and the way the frame is fixed down all need engineering for your plot. Soil in Bali varies from site to site, and a sloping block is different from a flat one. Your engineer looks at the site and sets the foundation type and size. ILO shows screw-pile foundation work in its photo galleries, but the right system for your villa is an engineering decision, not a sales choice.

The frame is then built to suit. Ask your builder to show how the frame is fixed down before work starts.

The foundation and the frame run at the same time

This is where steel helps your programme. ILO makes the wall panels, floor frames and roof trusses at its own factory in Banyuwangi, Java, while the site team prepares the ground and foundations. The two jobs overlap. When the frames arrive, the foundation is ready and the crew can start assembling.

In a traditional build the order is different. The frame cannot start until the foundations are cured, and the walls take months. With steel, a standard villa superstructure can be erected in days. The full sequence is set out in From factory to keys: the ILO build timeline.

Questions to ask any builder about foundations

  • Who designs the foundation, and is that engineer independent?
  • Is the foundation included in the price, or a separate allowance?
  • How is the frame fixed to the foundation?
  • Can foundation work start while the frame is being made?
  • What happens to the programme if the ground is different from what was expected?

Frequently asked questions

Does a steel frame villa need a foundation?

Yes. Every steel frame villa sits on a foundation. Lighter frames allow lighter foundations, but the foundation is still designed by your engineer for each site.

Does a lighter building use less foundation material?

It can. A steel frame is much lighter than concrete and block, so it puts less load on the ground. Lighter loads can allow simpler foundations, but your engineer sets the foundation design for each site.

Can the foundation be built while the frame is being made?

Yes. ILO makes the wall panels, floor frames and roof trusses at its factory in Banyuwangi, Java, while the site team prepares the ground and foundations. The two jobs overlap, which shortens the total programme.

Are foundations included in a frame kit price?

No. The Infinity Structural kit covers the agreed structural components only. Foundations and slabs, coverings, finishes, services, permits and professional design are outside the kit unless expressly agreed.

Who designs the foundation for my villa?

Your engineer. The ground, the footings and the way the frame is fixed down all need engineering for your plot, and the whole building must be tied to its foundation.
